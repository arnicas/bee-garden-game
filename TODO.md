### Seasons and water (next)

- [ ] Seasons and water across summers: see `docs/design/Seasons_Design.md` (Lynn, 2026-09-28). One slow **ground moisture** value carried from summer to summer; seasons (wet, ordinary, dry, hot and dry) that bias each day's weather; a meadow that shows it (grass colour and height, pools or dry hollows, dew, drooping or thriving flowers); friends that respond boldly (snails and mushrooms in wet summers, ants and aphids in dry ones); water as core play (thin dry grass shades less, pools are scarce, the hive asks for water on hot days, carried in the jar); and a small "Set up a climate scenario" button on the start screen with a summers chart on the results. Build order in the doc.
  - [x] Step 1 (2026-09-28): ground moisture carried over (`groundMoisture` in `garden.ts`, `nextMoisture()` in `weather.ts`: rain and a wet night raise it, heat and a dry night lower it, nearly half the way at most per summer). The grass follows it (`createMeadow(…, moisture)`: dry is shorter, patchy and straw-tinted, wet a little taller and deeper green); in a dry summer only about half the pools can fill, and they fill slower and dry faster (`puddles.setMoisture`); dew is heavier and lasts longer when wet, light and brief when dry; the summer start page says "A dry summer…" / "A wet summer…". Dev: `?moisture=0.1`. Tests: `tests/moisture.spec.ts`. Not yet: dry, cracked hollows where pools can't fill; the ground paint browning.
  - [x] Step 2 (2026-09-28): the season shapes the day. `Season` (wet, ordinary, dry, hot and dry) in `weather.ts`: `planWeather(random, season)` shifts the night odds, shower count and length, the hot spell's length and timing, and the chance of a second windy spell (an ordinary summer plans exactly the same days as before). `rollSeason()` rolls each summer's season, leaning toward the ground's moisture. The morning note leads with it ("A dry summer. Little rain, so water will be scarce."). Dev: `?season=wet|ordinary|dry|hotdry`. Tests: `tests/seasons.spec.ts`.
  - [x] Step 3 (2026-09-30): flowers and friends respond. Daisies droop in a dry summer (the head nods over, `droop` per flower) and some brown from the petal tips in (`browned`, a wilt wash in the petal shader); not the opening daisy. Each kind is nudged by next summer's moisture after pollination (`moistureFactor` in `meadow-plan.ts`: poppies ×1.25 dry, ×0.8 wet; cornflowers ×1.1 dry; daisies ×0.8 dry, ×1.15 wet), and the first summer's mix leans the same way. Friends change boldly (`friendCounts(all, moisture)`): snails from about 5 (dry) to 20 (wet), and they stay sealed more in the dry; aphids and so ladybirds up in the dry, down in the wet; butterflies more in the dry, fewer in the wet; caterpillars 4 to 8; ant mounds 6 dry, 3 wet, with more nectar raids in the dry; no mushrooms or fairy ring in a dry summer, clumps at most pools in a wet one. The start page's friends line names the cause in a dry or wet summer. An ordinary summer is unchanged. Tests: `tests/moisture.spec.ts`, `tests/summers.spec.ts`. Not yet: poppy petals spotted by rain; leaves smaller or curled in the dry. Also (2026-09-30): oat grass goes rusty brown in a dry summer, with more of it gone to seed.
  - [x] Step 4 (2026-09-30): water in play. Grass shade scales with the summer (`grassShade()` in `garden.ts`: full in an ordinary or wet summer, 0.3 at the driest), so thin, dry grass cools slowly and the notices and hints point to water instead. Pools hold groundwater (`puddles.setSprings`): each morning they start part full from the ground's moisture, deeper pools more, and the level sinks through the heat. On a hot day (a hot spell of 185 s or more, `hiveNeedsWater()` in `weather.ts`: about one ordinary day in five, most dry-summer days, every hot, dry one) the hive asks for water: 15% of the jar, carried by holding F at a pool or on wet petals, shown as a blue layer above the honey and a "Water 11 / 15%" line under the jar. It shares the crop with nectar, and the harvest isn't ready without it. The morning note says so, and the results' stats line says "Water to cool the hive". Off on test pages unless `?hivewater` (which also asks whatever the weather). Tests: `tests/hive-water.spec.ts`. Then (2026-09-30): the ground browns in a dry summer (`createGroundPaint().setMoisture` in `ground-paint.ts`: moss thins to dusty brown earth with fine cracks in the bare patches; a wet summer darkens it a little), and pools with no water show as pale, cracked clay beds with a darker rim (`puddles.hollows()` → `meadow.setHollows`: pools that can't fill this summer always, others once they dry out in a dry summer; none in an ordinary one). The results remember water across summers: "Water to cool the hive (2 of 3 hot days)". Still to tune in play: how often hot days come, the 15% request, and how long dry-summer pools last.
  - [x] Pollination holds the ground's water (2026-09-30). The meadow the bee leaves (next summer's flowers, from what was pollinated) now moves the ground moisture: `nextMoisture(m, plan, cover)` with `coverMoisture()` in `weather.ts`, where a meadow half as full loses about .11 more per summer (three patchy ordinary summers turn dry) and a fuller than normal one holds up to .04 more. Within the day, pools in a patchy meadow sink faster (`puddles.setBare`). The results say "Next summer: fewer poppies, drier ground." and the next start page "The meadow was patchy, so the ground dried out faster." (or the damp versions). This closes the loop: pollinate well, the meadow stays full, the ground holds water, the summers are gentler; dry summers favour poppies, so pollinating them is the way back.

  - Small weather ideas still open: nectar short early after a dry night; drops dry off the petals faster in the hot spell; butterflies and other insects drinking at puddle edges ("puddling"); Bee Vision glint on water.

### Meadow life
- [x] Bees in the grass (Lynn, 2026-10-08: helping a hive mate and fallen bees, built together; `src/grounded-bees.ts`, `tests/hive-mates.spec.ts`). Each day a tired forager rests on a leaf top (and a second at a pool's rim after rain or in heat), antennae swaying, wings shivering now and then. Land beside her and hold F: about 3 % of the jar goes to her over a couple of seconds (trophallaxis), then she grooms, warms her wings and flies off toward the hive, with a chime and "She's on her way home!". The results say "N hive mates helped home". Sometimes a fallen bee lies on her side, dull and still (cold rain by a pool, heat, or simply worn out), found by sight like the other friends, with a short note on what happened and what keeps a bee safe. A new Bee Facts topic, "Tired & fallen bees", covers the six-week life, chilled foragers, trophallaxis and why colonies are lost, and says plainly that sharing nectar in the field is the game's invention. `planGroundedBees()` takes a `stress` (0 to 1) that adds fallen bees; it is 0 until the summers arc feeds it.

- [ ] A Gaia-like meadow: weight the bee's impact by the friends (Lynn, 2026-09-30). Idea: how much the bee shapes next summer should depend on the meadow friends too, so the whole meadow is one system. To stay true to real bees, the friends should change the meadow through what they really do, and the bee's part is noticing and helping, not a score for sightings:
  - Friends act on the flowers each summer. Ladybirds eat aphids, so flowers with aphid clusters left set less seed and ladybirds protect them; butterflies pollinate a little too (small bonus to daisies and cornflowers); snails and caterpillars nibble leaves and seedlings (fewer daisies in a snail summer); ants carry some seeds (cornflower seeds have a fatty tip ants carry off, if that checks out), spreading seedlings.
  - Friends seen could weight the bee's own effect: e.g. a pollination "helped" bonus when the bee also met the ladybirds guarding those stems, or a meadow-health line on the results ("You met 9 friends: a lively meadow sets more seed."). Keep it gentle and explain it in words, so it reads as cause and effect, not points.
  - Loops to check: more flowers → more friends → more seed, and dry summers → ants and aphids, fewer snails; make sure nothing runs away (the bee should still lead).
  - Facts page: a short "the meadow is a web" page tying it together.

## Decisions held steady

- One outing per day; no multiple delivery trips or additional queen/hive happiness meter.
- Pollinating all three flower types is a richer meadow outcome, not a mandatory return gate.
- Simple laptop controls; no extra modifier-key combinations.
- Original procedural art/audio; reuse the meadow and keep elevated detail low.
- iPhone/iPad support and lupine are deferred.
- No spiders shown (only their webs); one snail species, no slugs; the Queen appears on the results screen only.
