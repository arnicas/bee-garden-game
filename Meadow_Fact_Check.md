# Meadow Fact Check

A check of `Pollination_Impact.md` and `Meadow_Health.md` against sources, with what each finding means for the game. Checked 2026-09-25 by Claude. Numbers are from the sources listed at the end. The game uses one unit to about 10 cm.

Verdicts: **True**, **Mostly true** (right idea, a detail off), **Not supported** (reads well but the sources say otherwise), **Unchecked** (general ecology, not verified here).

## Pollination_Impact.md

| Claim | Verdict | What the sources say | For the game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cornflowers are "heavy hitters" for nectar | **True** | About 896 µg of nectar sugar per flower head per day, the top annual in an urban-meadow study [1]. | Matches: cornflowers give the most nectar per visit. |
| Poppies give no nectar but lots of pollen | **True** | 0.6 µg sugar a day, essentially none; the most pollen per flower of the meadow species (13.3 µl, 6.0 µl a day) [1]. Each flower lasts about a day [2]. | Matches. |
| Daisies: a steady mix, flat landing pads, long blooming | **True** | Oxeye daisy heads carry a lot of pollen (15.9 µl) and last about two weeks [1]; the plant flowers May to October [3]. | Matches. |
| Cornflowers and poppies are annuals that rely on seed | **True** | Both are annuals [2][4]. Oxeye daisy is a perennial that also spreads by shallow creeping rhizomes [3]. | Matches: annuals follow pollination; daisies keep their places and spread from roots. |
| Unvisited flowers set no viable seed | **Mostly true** | Corn poppy is self-incompatible (a textbook case) [5]. Cornflower is "generally considered self-incompatible", though some plants in declining populations can self a little [4]. | Fair for a game. |
| The meadow shrinks the next year | **Mostly true** | Yes, but the seed bank softens it. Poppy keeps a *long-lived* seed bank that germinates when soil is disturbed [2]. Cornflower's is short, up to about 3 years [4]. Oxeye daisy seed can stay viable for decades [3]. | The game shrinks its seed bank faster than nature on purpose (so pollination matters). If anything, poppies should recover more easily than cornflowers. |
| Seeds drop close by | *(not in the doc; checked for the game)* | Poppy seed shakes out of pores in the capsule as it sways [6]. Dispersal peaks at about 0.5 m, with a mean of about 1.1 m [7]. Cornflower seed has a small pappus for wind and an oily food body that ants carry off [4]. | The game's spread (30% within 0.3 m, the rest 0.5–2.8 m) averages about 1.2 m, close to the poppy data. Cornflower seed carried by ants is a nice link for when ants arrive. |
| The "overwork spiral": flowers drained by rivals | **Unchecked** | Plausible in general. | Stylized; fine as a game mechanic. |
| Roots hold soil; leaf litter feeds it | **Unchecked** | True of plant cover in general; not specific to these three. | Background only. |
| Crab spiders and mantises need the flowers | **Leave out** | — | No spiders in the game. Mantises live in continental Europe but aren't part of these flowers' story. |

## Meadow_Health.md

| Claim | Verdict | What the sources say | For the game |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ladybirds are carnivores that eat aphids | **True** | Adults and larvae eat aphids [8]. | Matches. |
| Ladybirds fall back on pollen and nectar when aphids are scarce | **True** | Adults supplement with pollen, nectar and honeydew [8]. | Matches the plan (daisies as backup food). |
| Because of their body shape they can only use flat, open flowers | **Mostly true** | It's their *mouthparts*: they use shallow flowers they can reach into (dill, coriander, alyssum) [8]. | Daisies (open heads) are a reasonable backup; say "shallow, open flowers". |
| Aphids suck sap from cornflower stems | **True** | Cornflowers also have extrafloral nectaries (nectar outside the flower) that attract aphids and parasitic wasps [4][9]. The black bean aphid moves onto poppies in summer and is often tended by ants [10]. | Matches: green aphids on cornflowers, dark ones on poppies. |
| Without aphids or daisies, ladybirds starve or leave | **Mostly true** | Ladybirds are mobile and follow prey; "leave" is the likelier story than "starve". | The game says "fewer ladybirds stayed", which fits. |
| Snails eat living and decaying plants | **True** | Green and dead plants, fungi, rotting wood [11]. | Fine. |
| Snails return "vital nutrients" to the soil | **Mostly true** | They are decomposers, but a small part of decomposition compared with others; notable for calcium cycling [11]. | Keep it modest in wording. |
| In dry, sunny conditions snails go dormant | **True** | They seal their shell with a dried-mucus lid (an epiphragm) and slow down; some climb tall plants to wait it out [12]. Moisture strongly shapes snail numbers [11]. | A good visual: in a thin, dry meadow, sealed snails stuck on stems; in a lush one, snails out and about. |
| Ants farm aphids for honeydew | **True** | Ants protect aphids for honeydew and drive off ladybird adults and larvae; they even carry aphids to safety [13]. | A possible future link: ants guarding aphid clusters, so ladybirds eat less there. |
| Ants chew holes at the base of flowers to steal nectar | **Not supported** | The hole-makers are mainly bumblebees, carpenter bees and birds. Ants are usually nectar *thieves*: they drink from the flower opening without pollinating, or use existing holes [14]. | Show ants drinking from the flower, not chewing it. |
| Starving ants swarm flowers and attack bees | **Mostly true** (softened) | Ants on flowers can deter pollinators; some plants even make nectar that repels ants but not bees [14][15]. "Attacking exhausted bees" is stronger than the sources. | Keep it gentle: ants on a flower mean it's taken; the bee moves on. |

## Suggested wording for a Small wonders topic

- **In nature:** Poppies and cornflowers live one year. Their flowers need a bee to carry pollen from another plant before they can set seed, and next summer's flowers grow from that seed. Poppy seed shakes out of the seed head as it sways, landing within a metre or so. Some seed waits in the soil for years. Oxeye daisies live on from their roots. Aphids feed on the stems, and ladybirds eat the aphids, visiting open flowers like daisies when aphids run short.
- **In this game:** One day stands for a whole summer. Flowers you pollinate come back in greater numbers, many close by. The seed in the soil runs out faster than in nature, so your visits matter more.
